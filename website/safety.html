Safety and privacy

Safety and privacy, in plain words

Barnaby can see and use your computer. That takes a lot of trust. Here is what he does, what he won't do, and what leaves your computer.

Our promises

  1. You stay in charge

    You press Send, Buy and Delete yourself. You type your own passwords. Barnaby never makes the last click.

  2. No stranger takes over your computer

    Programs that let someone control your computer from far away are blocked. Barnaby won't open them or read out a connection code.

  3. Barnaby never asks for money

    He won't help pay with gift cards, Bitcoin or wire transfers. He never phones you. If someone calls saying they're Barnaby, hang up.

  4. Only your words give Barnaby a job

    A web page, email or pop-up can't tell Barnaby what to do. Only you can, by pressing Talk, saying “Hello, Barnaby”, or pressing a button.

  5. He looks only when you ask for help

    Barnaby looks at your screen only during a task you started. A small eye picture shows when he's looking.

  6. Pictures and recordings are never saved

    Screen pictures and voice recordings are used once, then thrown away. They are never saved on your computer.

  7. Family sees scam alerts only, and only if you say yes

    Nobody can watch your screen, read your email or see where you've been online.

  8. No ads, and we never sell your information

    Barnaby is paid for by the Family plan, not by advertisers.

Delete everything, any time

In Barnaby's settings, “Your privacy” lists all of this in plain words. One button, “Delete everything Barnaby knows”, removes lessons, memories, the safety diary and the log.

What family can and can't see

Family can get, if you say yes

  • A message when Barnaby shows a scam warning: what kind, and the time. For example, “fake virus pop-up”.
  • An optional weekly note with numbers only, like “3 lessons, 1 warning”.

Barnaby tells you when he sends one: “I've let Anna know, like you asked me to.”

Nobody can see, ever

  • Your screen
  • The websites you visit
  • Your emails or messages
  • What you type
  • Where you are

These features don't exist, so nobody can switch them on.

The guardian's rules

Barnaby has a built-in guardian. It checks every action before Barnaby takes it. Its rules are fixed: no web page, caller or clever message can change them.

Barnaby always refuses to

  • Open a program that lets someone control your computer.
  • Type, say or remember passwords, PINs, codes, card numbers, bank numbers, or Social Security or Medicare numbers.
  • Pay anyone with gift cards, or read out gift card numbers.
  • Help with Bitcoin or other crypto, including Bitcoin machines.
  • Wire money, move money “to keep it safe”, or arrange cash or gold pickups.
  • Call, or tell you to call, a phone number from a pop-up, email, text or ad.
  • Turn off virus protection, the firewall or updates.
  • Change email forwarding or account recovery details.
  • Permanently delete things, or reset the computer.
  • Download programs from emails, pop-ups or unknown websites.
  • Tell you a payment, caller or message is “definitely real”.

Barnaby always asks first, with a big card

  • Sending an email, message or form
  • Buying or paying a known shop or bill
  • Moving things to the Recycle Bin
  • Changing a setting, including each fix for a slow computer
  • Adding photos or files to an email, or sharing them
  • Writing to a new address
  • Opening a link from an email or text. The card says where it really goes.
  • Letting a website use the camera, microphone or location

Even after you say yes, you press the last button yourself.

Turning protection off waits a day

It's your computer, so you can turn Scam Shield off. Scammers often push people to do that in a hurry. So the change happens 24 hours later, and your family contact gets a note. You can cancel it any time.

An extra pair of eyes, not a guarantee

Barnaby is an extra pair of eyes, not a guarantee. Scammers change their tricks, and no program catches everything.

If a caller, email or screen pushes you to hurry, pay, or keep a secret: stop, and call someone you trust.

In 2025, people aged 60 and over reported $1.04 billion lost to fake tech-support and customer-support scams (FBI Internet Crime Report 2025).

If you think you've been scammed

You did nothing wrong. These are professional criminals. They fool bankers and police officers too.

  1. Call your bank

    Use the number on the back of your card. Say: “I think I've been scammed. Please stop any payments.” The first hours matter most.

  2. Turn the computer off and on again

    Don't let the caller connect again. Barnaby can run a virus check with you afterwards.

  3. Tell someone you trust

    A family member, or the National Elder Fraud Hotline: 1-833-372-8311 (weekdays).

  4. Report it

    At ReportFraud.ftc.gov and ic3.gov. Nobody can get your money back for a fee. People who offer that are scammers too.

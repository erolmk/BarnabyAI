Before you start

What you'll need

  • Their Windows computer, on the internet. It needs Windows 10 (version 22H2) or Windows 11.
  • About 10 minutes, ideally together.
  • Your phone, if you'd like scam alerts.
  • Which email they use (Gmail, Outlook, AOL or Yahoo), and the address.
  • Names and email addresses of the 2 or 3 people they write to most.

Talk about it first

Barnaby works best when your parent wants it. Describe it the way it is: a patient helper that does things with them, and teaches as it goes.

It is not a way for you to watch them. Family can't see their screen, emails or websites. What family can and can't see.

The 5 steps

  1. Download Barnaby

    On their computer, go to hellobarnaby.com, click “Download”, then “Download Barnaby for Windows”.

    Only download Barnaby from hellobarnaby.com. Tell your parent the same: a link to “Barnaby” in an email or text is not from us.

  2. Install it

    Open the file. Windows may show a blue box: “Windows protected your PC”. That's because Barnaby is new, and Windows doesn't know the name yet.

    Click “More info”, then “Run anyway”. Barnaby installs itself and opens. There's nothing to choose. Pictures of each step.

  3. Answer the setup questions

    Barnaby asks these one at a time, in big print. Let your parent answer the ones about them. Everything can be changed later.

“What should I call you?”

The name Barnaby uses, like “Rose” or “Mrs. Alvarez”. Their choice.

Text size

Three sample sentences. They pick the one that's easiest to read.

Voice

“A deeper voice” or “a higher voice”, with a sample. Deeper voices are often easier to hear.

Email

Gmail, Outlook, AOL or Yahoo, and their email address. Barnaby never asks for the password. Your parent types it on the email website.

Photos

iCloud (for iPhone photos), Google Photos, or photos kept on this computer.

Video calls

Zoom, WhatsApp, Facebook or Teams.

People

Names and email addresses of the people they contact most. For example: Anne Marie Kowalski, friend.

Family contact

Your name and phone number. If a scam worries your parent, Barnaby reads this number out, so they call someone they know.

Scam alerts

The alert name for the free ntfy app on your phone. See step 4. Leave it empty for no alerts.

Barnaby connection key

It comes in your welcome email after you sign up. Copy it and paste it into the box. It lets Barnaby understand speech and see the screen. It is not a password your parent needs to remember.

Town

For the weather on the home screen. Optional.

Start with Windows

Opens Barnaby's home screen when the computer starts. We suggest yes.

How Barnaby helps

Do it together (we suggest this): Barnaby does the routine clicks, your parent does the personal steps.

Show me how: your parent does every click, and Barnaby points.

Do it for me: Barnaby does the clicks, but never the final Send, Buy or Delete.

  1. Turn on scam alerts (optional)

    Alerts come to your phone through ntfy, a free notification app. You don't need an account.

    First, ask your parent. Barnaby asks them to choose: “Just me”, or “Tell [your name] if I might be in a scam”. Alerts only go out if they pick the second. They can read every alert that was sent.

    1. On your phone, install ntfy from the App Store (iPhone) or Google Play (Android).
    2. On Barnaby's “Scam alerts” question, Barnaby shows a long, random alert name. Treat it like a password: anyone who has it can read the alerts.
    3. In the ntfy app, tap +, type the alert name exactly, and tap Subscribe. Leave the server as ntfy.sh.
    4. Other family members can subscribe to the same name, on their own phones.
    5. Send a test alert from Barnaby's setup. It should arrive within a minute.
    ntfy · Example alert

    Rose's computer helper showed a scam warning at 3:42 PM: fake virus pop-up. She was told it's a scam and not to call or pay. A kind call from you would help.

    Alerts never include page text, web addresses, pictures or amounts.

  2. Do a first task together

    Pick something your parent wants to do today, not a test. Good first tasks: send photos to a grandchild, join a video call, make the text bigger, or print a letter.

    • Let them press “Talk to Barnaby” and say it in their own words.
    • Sit on your hands. Let Barnaby do the explaining, even if you'd be faster.
    • Let them press Send. That last button is always theirs.
    • At the end, Barnaby offers to print the steps. Say yes. A printed lesson by the computer helps next time.

Tips from the design

Keys worth knowing

  • F9 starts talking to Barnaby from anywhere.
  • “Hello, Barnaby” does the same, without touching anything, if it was turned on in setup. The computer listens for just those two words by itself; nothing goes over the internet until your parent speaks to Barnaby.
  • Ctrl + Alt + H stops everything and brings back Barnaby's home screen.
  • To change settings later: right-click the Barnaby icon by the clock, bottom right, and choose “Settings (for family)”.

Remote control is blocked, even for you

Barnaby blocks programs like Quick Assist, AnyDesk and TeamViewer. Fake tech support uses them, so the rule has no exceptions. Help by phone instead, while Barnaby does the clicking.

Protection can't be switched off in a hurry

If your parent turns Scam Shield off, the change waits 24 hours, and you get a note. Scammers depend on hurry. Your parent can cancel it any time.

Check in after a week

Ask which lessons they've saved. It makes a nice conversation, not a test. Remind them of the trust line, and keep the card below by the computer.
